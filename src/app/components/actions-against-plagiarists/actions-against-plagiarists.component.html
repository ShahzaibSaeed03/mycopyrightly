Here's an overview of where you can file legal action against a copyright infringing party in the USA, European Union, and the UK, including specialized courts like the CCB and IPEC:
  1. Copyright Claims Board (CCB):
    • What it is: A tribunal under the U.S. Copyright Office for small copyright claims.
    • Jurisdiction: Claims of up to $30,000. Participation is voluntary for both parties.
    • When to use: Ideal for resolving smaller disputes cost-effectively without going to federal court.
    • How to file: File online through the CCB portal.
  2. Federal Court:
    • What it is: The primary venue for copyright infringement cases in the U.S.
    • Jurisdiction: Exclusive jurisdiction over copyright infringement cases.
    • When to use: For significant cases, higher damages, or when injunctive relief is sought.
    • Where to file: File in the federal district court where the defendant resides, conducts business, or where the infringement occurred.
  1. National Courts in EU Member States:
    • What they are: Each EU member state has its own national courts handling copyright infringement.
    • Jurisdiction: Typically, the courts where the infringement occurred or where the defendant is domiciled.
    • When to use: Cases must be filed under the national laws of the member state, harmonized by EU directives.
    • Example: In Germany, cases are filed in specialized chambers for copyright disputes.
  2. Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU):
    • What it is: Not a first-instance court for disputes but can interpret EU copyright law.
    • When to use: Only accessible through national courts referring questions on EU law.
  1. Intellectual Property Enterprise Court (IPEC):
    • What it is A specialized court for intellectual property disputes, including copyright.
    • Jurisdiction: Claims up to £500,000; offers streamlined procedures and capped costs.
    • When to use: Ideal for small to medium-sized disputes or cases requiring expedited handling.
    • Where to file: IPEC is part of the High Court of England and Wales.
  2. High Court (Chancery Division):
    • What it is: The main venue for high-value or complex copyright cases.
    • Jurisdiction: No monetary cap, broader procedural rules than IPEC.
    • When to use: For large-scale infringement cases or those exceeding IPEC's jurisdictional limits.

Factors to Consider

  • Jurisdiction: File in the country where the infringement occurred or where the infringer resides.
  • Costs: Courts like the CCB and IPEC offer cost-effective alternatives for smaller claims.
  • Legal Advice: Consulting an intellectual property attorney in the relevant jurisdiction is recommended for complex cases.
