What Is Copyright?

Copyright is a legal right that protects the original works of creators. When you create something unique, like a story, a song, or a design, copyright grants you the exclusive right to use, distribute, and benefit from your work. It ensures that your creativity and effort are recognized and safeguarded from unauthorized use.

Where Does Copyright Apply?

Copyright laws exist in nearly every country worldwide, though the specifics may vary. International agreements, such as the Berne Convention, ensure that copyright protection extends across borders in over 180 member countries, including the United States, the European Union, and the United Kingdom. This means your rights are respected internationally, providing broad coverage for your creative works.

What Can Copyright Protect?

Here’s an exhaustive list of the types of creative works that can be protected by copyright, depending on the jurisdiction and specific laws:

1. Literary Works Novels, short stories, and novellas Poems and poetry collections Essays and articles Scripts and screenplays Academic papers, theses, and dissertations Computer programs and software code (in some jurisdictions)

2. Musical Works Songs (lyrics and melody) Instrumental compositions Sheet music and musical notations Arrangements and adaptations of existing music

3. Dramatic Works Plays and stage performances Dramatic scripts Choreographic works (if recorded or notated)

4. Visual Artworks Paintings and drawings Photographs and photo collages Sculptures and statues Engravings and prints Architectural works (building designs, blueprints)

5. Audiovisual Works Movies and films TV shows and series Documentaries Video clips and vlogs Animations and cartoons

6. Sound Recordings Recorded performances of music Audiobooks Podcasts Spoken-word performances

7. Broadcasts Television broadcasts Radio programs Online streaming broadcasts

8. Industrial and Commercial Designs Print-on-demand (POD) designs for merchandise Logos and brand elements (if sufficiently creative) Product packaging designs

9. Digital and Multimedia Works Websites (content and design) Video games (including storyline, graphics, and music) User interfaces (UI) and icons Software and applications (code and user experience design) NFTs (if they embody a creative work)

10. Written and Recorded Communications Letters and emails (if creative and original) Diaries and journals Public speeches and oratory

11. Works of Applied Art Furniture designs Fashion designs (if unique and creative) Jewelry designs

12. Compilations and Derivative Works Compilations of data or content (e.g., anthologies, databases) Translations of original works Adaptations and reinterpretations of existing works

13. Emerging and AI-Generated Works AI-generated text, images, or music (if the jurisdiction allows it and human authorship is involved) AI prompts designed to produce specific outputs

14. Miscellaneous Maps and cartographic materials Architectural models Pantomimes Calligraphy and typography designs (if original and creative) Heraldic designs and emblems

Important Notes

  • Originality is Key: To be copyrightable, the work must be original and show some degree of creativity or intellectual effort.
  • Fixed in a Tangible Medium: Most jurisdictions require the work to be fixed in a tangible form (written, recorded, or saved digitally).
  • Non-Copyrightable Works: Facts, ideas, procedures, and common knowledge are not protected by copyright, though the expression of these elements may be.
